What is Chaldean numerology, and how does it differ from Pythagorean?

Chaldean numerology traces back to ancient Babylon (Chaldea), making it one of the oldest documented numeral systems used to study the vibration of names. Rather than assigning numbers in simple alphabetical order, Chaldean numerology groups letters by the sound and energy they carry — which is why the sequence of letters under each number looks irregular at first glance.

The most distinctive rule in the Chaldean system is that the number 9 is considered sacred and is never assigned to any single letter. It only ever appears as a result of addition, never as a starting value — a detail that separates it clearly from the newer Pythagorean system.

Pythagorean numerology, developed centuries later in ancient Greece, assigns letters in a clean 1-to-9 sequence following the alphabet (A=1, B=2, C=3...). It's simpler to calculate but, according to numerology tradition, less precise about the underlying vibration of a name, since it ignores the phonetic weight of letters. Chaldean numerology is generally preferred for analyzing names, while Pythagorean numerology is more commonly used for birth-date calculations like Life Path numbers.
